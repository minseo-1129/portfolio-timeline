RECO / Reflective Companion

Designing Summaries as Part of the Interaction

A generative summary can become memory, a transparency mechanism, and a negotiated boundary between private reflection and human care.

Period · Feb 2026 – May 2026 Type · Generative AI · Summarisation · Reflection · Human–AI Care Participants · 12 young people · 8 link workers
Research question

When a reflective conversation ends, what should persist — and who should control that representation?

01 · PERSISTENCE

What remains after a reflective conversation?

The Reflective Companion was initially designed around dialogue, but dialogue disappears quickly.

A young person may articulate something meaningful and then need to remember, reinterpret, or communicate it days later. This made the generated summary more than a post-processing feature: it could shape what becomes persistent after the interaction ends.

02 · WORKFLOW

Making AI interpretation visible and negotiable

The system generated a structured summary after each reflective conversation. Crucially, that output was not silently stored as truth.

01ConversationYoung person reflects with the agent.
02GenerateAgent creates a structured summary.
03ReviewUser sees what the agent understood.
04EditUser can request changes.
05ConfirmOnly approved content persists.
06ShareConfirmed summary becomes visible to the link worker.

The summary exposed the agent’s interpretation of the user and made that interpretation contestable before it became memory or entered the wider care system.

03 · ROLES

Four roles emerged from the summary

Testing with 12 young people and eight link workers suggested that the summary was experienced as more than a record of previous dialogue.

01Shared & calibrated memoryReduce repeated explanation and support continuity.
02Transparency & data agencyMake the agent’s interpretation inspectable, editable, negotiable.
03Relational engagementAn accurate summary could retrospectively signal that the agent had listened.
04Mediated visibilityA middle layer between a fully private transcript and complete practitioner visibility.
04 · BOUNDARY

A summary can mediate visibility between private reflection and human care

Full transcriptMaximum visibility, but potentially too much disclosure for a private reflective conversation.
User-confirmed summaryA reduced representation supporting continuity and oversight without automatically exposing every raw detail.

The design question is not only what the model can summarise. It is who can inspect, correct, approve, store, and later access that representation.

05 · NEXT

The next question: which summary fits which conversation?

The findings led to a follow-up design question: should every conversation receive the same kind of summary?

Self-understanding summaryDecision-support summary
Emotional conversationEmotion × understandingSupport interpretation of why an experience felt the way it did.Emotion × decisionTranslate an emotional exploration toward possible next steps.
Practical conversationPractical × understandingHelp reveal what a practical problem says about preferences, needs, or barriers.Practical × decisionOrganise options, plans, and actionable next steps.

This 2×2 is an ongoing follow-up study design, not a finding from the completed summary study.

The emerging question is whether a summary is more useful when its function matches the support a person was seeking from the conversation.

Design takeaway

A generative summary is not just a record; it can extend meaningful reflection beyond the conversation by making the agent’s interpretation visible, editable, and persistent.
