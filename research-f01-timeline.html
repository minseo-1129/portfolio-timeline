ForMe · Dialogue Design

Designing ForMe's Task-Based Dialogue Flow

Translating Goal–Plan–Do–Check into conversations that help children remember, perform, recover, and recognise success in everyday routines.

Period · Mar 2022 – Mar 2024 Type · Dialogue Design · Conversational Agent · Field Deployment Participants · Children aged 6–7 with ADHD and mild/borderline intellectual functioning
Research question

How can a conversational agent scaffold everyday tasks without taking control of the task away from the child?

01 · PRINCIPLE

From self-instruction to conversation

ForMe wasn't designed simply to remind children what to do — the conversation itself needed to support remembering, planning, acting, and recognising completion.

GoalRecall what needs to be done.
PlanPrepare how to begin.
DoPerform the task.
CheckConfirm completion.
02 · TRANSLATION

A reusable dialogue grammar

Individual flows for routines like reading, diary-writing, and getting ready for bed followed a shared conversational shape:

ContextRecallRepairPlan / DoCheckSelf-affirmReward
03 · REPAIR

Designing for breakdown

The useful interaction wasn't the ideal path — it was the system's ability to keep the child moving when memory, motivation, or speech recognition broke down, by progressively adding support rather than collapsing every failure into the same fallback.

04 · MASTERY

Closing the loop with mastery

Completion was made deliberately visible — children were prompted to acknowledge "I did it on my own," connecting behaviour to their own sense of competence.

05 · DEPLOYMENT

From dialogue flows to everyday use

The system was deployed in an eight-week home pilot.

8/10completed study
8 wksdeployment
76.1%task compliance

Small pilot study — psychological outcomes should be interpreted cautiously.

Design takeaway

Conversation design for children's self-regulation starts with understanding the full range of ways a child may respond — not only the ideal response.
