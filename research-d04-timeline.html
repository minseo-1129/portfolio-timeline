REDI

From Self-Recording to Self-Regulation

A four-week deployment revealed how adolescents turn personal data into decisions — and how uncertainty can interrupt that process.

Period · Dec 2023 – Apr 2024 Type · Mobile Deployment · Digital Phenotyping · Self-Regulation · Field Study Participants · 36 adolescents · 17 intervention / 19 control
Research question

What happens after someone sees their own data?

01 · QUESTION

Building the working REDI changed the research question

Once adolescents could sustain recording, the more interesting question was no longer whether data could be collected — it was what happened after someone saw their own data.

Observation“I slept badly and felt stressed.”
Self-regulation“I understand what may have contributed to it, I know what I could try, and I can judge whether that change helped.”

Seventeen adolescents in the intervention group used the working application for four weeks, combining active records, passive digital phenotype data, and routine-aware mood EMA prompts.

02 · PROCESS

Self-regulation looked like personal experimentation

Participants didn’t simply receive a recommendation and follow it. They interpreted records, considered explanations, tried changes, observed what happened, and revised their strategy.

ObserveWhat happened?
InterpretWhy might it have happened?
ChooseWhat could I change?
TryTest a change in daily life.
ReviewDid it make a difference?
Lifestyle behaviour“Is what I am doing okay?”
Emotion“Why did I feel this way?”

The sequence can repeat: reviewing an outcome creates new observations and another opportunity to adjust.

03 · UNCERTAINTY

Uncertainty interrupted every stage

The recurring barrier wasn’t simply lack of information. It was uncertainty about how to interpret and act on that information.

PhaseUncertainty typeQuestion
ObserveEvaluationIs this behaviour appropriate, or is something wrong?
InterpretExplanationWhat caused this emotional state, and which contextual factors matter?
Choose / TryActionWhat is realistically changeable, and what change should I try?
ReviewOutcomeWhat should I expect, has progress occurred, and did my effort contribute?

05 · IMPLICATION

Design around the next decision, not only the last record

Collecting data isn’t necessarily psychologically neutral. Repeated questioning, recording, reviewing, and interpreting can themselves become part of how a person reflects on and manages daily life.

Reporting mindsetHere is what happened.
Self-regulation mindsetWhat does this person still need to understand before they can decide what to do next?
Design takeaway

A self-tracking system should be designed around what a person still needs to understand before deciding what to do next.
