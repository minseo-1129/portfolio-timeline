What Should a Personalised Health Report Actually Personalise?

Adolescents wanted different kinds of feedback for sleep, study, exercise, and leisure — suggesting that personalisation begins before the message is generated.

Period · Mar 2023 – Jun 2023 Type · Intelligent Visualisation · Card Sorting Participants · 14 Korean adolescents aged 14–18
Research question

What exactly should the system personalise when it turns lifestyle data into visualisation and feedback?

01 · FRAMING

Personalisation is more than changing the wording

When personal data become feedback, the first personalisation decision may happen before any message is generated.

Sleep happens every day. Exercise may happen only a few times a week. Study may be judged against a personally meaningful goal. For leisure, an adolescent may not even know what an appropriate reference point should be.

Common framingPersonalise the wording of one report.
Emerging framingDecide what kind of report makes sense for this behaviour before writing it.
02 · STUDY

Building reports from participants’ own data

Fourteen Korean adolescents aged 14–18 recorded four lifestyle domains for two weeks. We then produced different report structures from their own records and used card sorting and interviews to compare what was useful.

DomainSleepHighly repetitive, naturally suited to daily summaries.
DomainStudyOften evaluated against a personal goal.
DomainExerciseOccurs less frequently, better understood over a longer window.
DomainLeisureMay benefit from an external reference when personal standards are unclear.
03 · REPRESENTATION

The useful representation changed by behaviour

A daily sleep pattern and occasional exercise activity shouldn’t necessarily be summarised, compared, or evaluated using the same information structure.

DecisionExamples participants compared
Summary levelDaily vs weekly
ComparisonRecent three-day trend · week-to-week · weekday vs weekend
EvaluationPersonal target vs external recommendation
InterpretationAdditional patterns that help explain what the data may mean

Participants often preferred their own goals for study and exercise, while objective guidance was more useful for some leisure behaviours where an appropriate amount was less obvious.

04 · MEANING

Interpretation mattered more than another graph

Participants were especially interested when feedback revealed a pattern difficult to see directly in raw records.

Base layerRecorded dataSleep, study, exercise, leisure, and their timing.
DisplaySummaries, trends, comparisonsMake behaviour visible across meaningful time windows and reference points.
Interpretation“What does this pattern mean for me?”Built on display, not an equal alternative to it.
05 · DECISION

Personalisation as a sequence of decisions

DecisionQuestion
DomainWhat behaviour is this?
FrequencyHow often does it naturally occur?
TimescaleWhat window is meaningful?
ReferencePersonal target or external recommendation?
InterpretationWhat pattern would actually help?

Only after those choices does language generation become the final layer of personalisation.

Design takeaway

Personalisation shouldn’t stop at representing a person through data and numbers; meaningful behaviour change requires understanding the values that give those data meaning.
